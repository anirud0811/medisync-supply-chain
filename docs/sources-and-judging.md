# Sources and judging notes

## Supply-chain problem framing

- [World Health Organization: notification systems for shortages and stockouts of medicines and vaccines](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-EMP-IAU-2017.15) — describes shortage/stockout notification and public-health considerations.
- [World Health Organization: substandard and falsified medical products](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/substandard-and-falsified-medical-products) — background on risks, prevention, detection, and supply-chain complexity.
- [World Health Organization: procurement, supply chain, and inventory management](https://www.who.int/tools/quality-management-system-for-non-laboratory-settings/pillar-3--procurement-supply-chain-and-inventory-management) — guidance on stock levels, consumption, expiry, distribution, and replenishment processes.

## Common student hackathon evaluation themes

- [Devpost: understanding hackathon submission and judging criteria](https://info.devpost.com/blog/understanding-hackathon-submission-and-judging-criteria) — explains commonly used dimensions such as technical implementation, ease of use, demo, potential impact, idea quality, and design.
- [University of Missouri TigerHacks: judging rubrics](https://tigerhacks.missouri.edu/rubrics_all.pdf) — includes a beginner rubric covering creativity, functionality, user experience, usefulness, learning, and presentation.
- [HackCC: student hackathon criteria](https://hackcc-2025.devpost.com/rules) — example criteria include technical complexity, potential impact, design/execution, and presentation.

**Important:** these references illustrate recurring themes; they do not predict the user’s specific instructor or event rubric. Always follow the current assignment brief and official event criteria first.
